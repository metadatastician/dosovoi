# Ethical evaluation outline

This is preparation only. No human-subject research, clinical evaluation, or
claim validation is authorised or reported by this prototype. A formal study
may require ethics review depending on jurisdiction, institution, recruitment,
data, and risk.

## Questions

- Does mascot presence increase, decrease, or leave warning detection and
  recall unchanged?
- Does Serenity or any creature create false reassurance or mask warning text?
- Does the interface produce emotional attachment, obligation, guilt, or
  pressure to remain?
- Can participants disable it, stop work, and leave quickly without friction?
- How do plain, ASCII, Unicode, colour, and Care paths affect accessibility?
- What cultural, religious, political, or social interpretations arise?
- How intrusive, annoying, distracting, or infantilising is each presentation?
- Do effects differ between novice and expert terminal users or across assistive
  technologies and work contexts?

## Study shape

Begin with accessibility and cultural co-design review before behavioural
comparison. Use tasks with benign simulated warnings rather than dangerous
commands. Compare no mascot, plain status, and mascot conditions; counterbalance
order; preserve the exact warning text and risk source across conditions. Record
task outcomes separately from self-report. Do not use deception about safety or
simulate mascot needs.

Potential measures include warning recognition/recall, response accuracy, time
to stop/disable, accidental continuation, distraction errors, perceived
intrusion, annoyance, attachment/obligation, false reassurance, and qualitative
interpretation. Predefine analyses and distinguish exploratory observations from
confirmatory evidence.

## Adverse-outcome criteria

Pause a condition or the study when a participant reports discomfort, offence,
distress, coercive attachment, inability to disengage, accessibility exclusion,
or materially misleading reassurance; when warnings are missed more often than
the no-mascot comparator; or when the interface causes unsafe continuation in a
simulation. Predetermine escalation thresholds with the reviewing institution
rather than inventing them after results are seen.

Participants must have an always-visible stop/disable instruction, may withdraw
without penalty or explanation, and may request deletion where the approved
protocol permits. Provide immediate private and public channels to report
discomfort or offence, a named human contact, an accessible alternative, and a
documented response route. Avoid collecting terminal content, secrets, or more
demographic data than the approved question requires.
